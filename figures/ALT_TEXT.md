## Figure1

Fig. 1. Embedding anisotropy and its correction. (A) Cumulative variance explained by the ranked principal directions of the reference embeddings; the dashed line marks 50%. (B, C) Paired bootstrap change in protein-centric Fmax relative to untransformed embeddings, for centering and for whitening, over all benchmark queries (B) and over the orphan subset (C). Bars are 95% percentile intervals from 2000 paired resamples; filled markers indicate intervals excluding zero.

Alt text: Three-panel line and interval plot. Panel A shows cumulative variance against ranked embedding dimension on a logarithmic axis for three protein language models, each reaching half of total variance within a small number of dimensions (ESM-2 2, ProtT5 23, ProstT5 5). Panels B and C show change in Fmax relative to untransformed embeddings with 95% confidence intervals: whitening gives a clear positive shift in both panels, whereas centering alone gives a small shift over all queries and none on orphans.

## Figure2

Fig. 2. Annotation accuracy as homologous evidence is withdrawn. (A) Protein-centric Fmax for whitened embeddings across identity-stratified conditions, from all queries (T0) through progressively stricter identity ceilings. The rightmost column is the orphan subset: the queries for which BLAST-class search returns no hit at all, with its size in parentheses. It is plotted detached and on a shaded background because it is a subset of the queries rather than a further exclusion applied to all of them, so the denominator differs from every other column and no trend should be read across the gap. Figure 4 shows what becomes of this subset under a more sensitive search. Error bars are 95% bootstrap intervals. (B) Fmax by Gene Ontology aspect for the best-performing model, over all queries (solid) and over orphans (hatched); the corresponding values for the other models are given in Table 1.

Alt text: Two-panel figure. Panel A plots Fmax against increasingly strict homology exclusion for three protein language models; all three decline gradually and remain well above zero on the orphan subset, which is drawn as a detached point because it rests on fewer proteins. Panel B is a grouped bar chart of Fmax split by ontology aspect for one model, with cellular component highest, molecular function intermediate and biological process lowest, and every aspect scoring lower on orphans than on the full query set.

## Figure3

Fig. 3. Reach, not accuracy, is what embedding transfer adds. (A) Fmax for embedding transfer and for two homology searches across the identity-stratified conditions, all transferring at the same significance threshold. As in Figure 2, the orphan column is a subset of the queries and is shown detached on a shaded background; a break in a line marks a condition for which that method has no measurement. (B) Fraction of queries for which each search finds any permitted hit. (C) Paired difference in Fmax between embedding transfer and profile search, over all queries and over only those the profile search could answer; filled markers indicate 95% intervals excluding zero.

Alt text: Three-panel figure comparing embedding-based transfer with sequence-homology search. Panel A shows Fmax falling for both homology searches as identity ceilings tighten while embedding transfer declines far less. Panel B shows the fraction of queries each homology search can answer at all, falling steeply for the stricter conditions. Panel C shows that the advantage over all queries largely disappears when restricted to queries the profile search could answer, indicating the gain comes from coverage rather than accuracy.

## Figure4

Fig. 4. The orphan subset withstands a more sensitive search. (A) Number of benchmark proteins retaining orphan status as the iterative-profile E-value threshold is relaxed; the dashed horizontal line is the count from BLAST-class search alone and the vertical line marks the threshold adopted. (B) Fmax with 95% bootstrap intervals for the original orphan subset, the subset surviving profile search, and the proteins removed by it.

Alt text: Two-panel figure. Panel A shows the number of proteins still classed as orphans decreasing as the profile-search E-value threshold is relaxed, with a reference line for the count from BLAST-class search alone. Panel B shows Fmax with confidence intervals for three nested subsets; the hardened subset scores slightly lower than the original and the removed proteins score higher, and all three intervals overlap.

## Figure5

Fig. 5. Application to STAT-dependent uncharacterized genes. (A) Percentage of genes whose expression depends on STAT, by expression decile, for genes with and without a functional description in the reference annotation. (B) Effect size of the difference in fold-change distributions within each decile; filled markers are significant at p < 0.05 and ρ is the Spearman correlation of absolute effect size with expression. (C) Gene Ontology aspects assigned to the target proteins by calibrated embedding transfer; counts above each bar.

Alt text: Three-panel figure. Panel A shows the percentage of STAT-dependent genes rising with expression for both gene classes, with described genes consistently higher than uncharacterized genes and the gap widening at high expression. Panel B shows the effect size of that difference becoming more negative as expression increases, with a positive Spearman correlation between absolute effect size and expression. Panel C shows the proportion of target proteins receiving molecular function, biological process, or only cellular component terms.

## Figure6

Fig. 6. Structural reach and biophysical character of the dark proteome. (A) Annotation accuracy under three nested definitions of an orphan protein, each adding one search that must also return nothing; the shaded field holds the proteins each step removed, scored separately. Error bars are 95% percentile bootstrap intervals. (B) Cross-tabulation of sequence homology against structural reach for the target proteins, showing that the two searches recover overlapping but distinct sets. (C) Proportion of proteins falling in a structural cluster, against a background matched on length alone and against one matched additionally on predicted 3Di entropy; odds ratios with 95% intervals and Fisher exact P. (D) Charge–hydropathy distribution with the Uversky boundary; points above the line satisfy the criterion for intrinsic disorder. The boundary and both percentages are arithmetic over the amino-acid sequence and involve no language model.

Alt text: Four-panel figure. Panel A shows annotation accuracy under progressively stricter orphan definitions, with the proteins excluded at each step scored separately in a shaded region at the right and scoring lower than the sets they were removed from. Panel B shows horizontal stacked bars for target proteins with and without a sequence homologue, each split by whether a structural relative was found; a substantial number of proteins with a sequence homologue have no structural hit and vice versa. Panel C shows that the deficit in structural clustering for the dark set relative to its background persists when the background is additionally matched on predicted structure quality. Panel D is a scatter plot of net charge against hydropathy in which the dark proteins sit disproportionately above the disorder boundary compared with the background cloud. Structural relatives were found for 18 of 70 previously orphan proteins.

## FigureS1

Fig. S1. Phylogenetic distribution of detectable homology. (A) Cumulative proportion of genes whose furthest detectable homologue falls at or before each clade rank, for the STAT-dependent uncharacterized targets, a control of uncharacterized genes that are not STAT-dependent, and a length-matched whole-proteome background. The shaded field marks the ranks counted as lineage-restricted. (B) Proportion lineage-restricted in each set, with Wilson 95% intervals and the two odds ratios. The enrichment against the whole proteome is substantial, and the enrichment against uncharacterized genes that are not STAT-dependent is not detectable, which places the restriction with the absence of annotation rather than with the transcriptional response. The dashed mark on the target bar, where present, is the proportion remaining once proteins with a structural relative beyond the restricted ranks are removed. Only the target set was searched structurally, so this mark is not comparable with the other two bars, which carry the same over-calling uncorrected; it bounds the target figure from below rather than ranking the three sets. Strata rest on detectable sequence homology, and absence of detection is not absence of a homologue.

Alt text: Two-panel supplementary figure. Panel A shows cumulative distributions of the furthest clade at which a homologue is detected; the target and control curves rise steeply within the arthropod ranks and track each other closely, while the proteome background rises more gradually and reaches high cumulative values only at the deepest ranks. Panel B shows three bars of the lineage-restricted percentage with confidence intervals: the targets and the uncharacterized control are similar and overlapping, and both are higher than the proteome background. A dashed line within the target bar marks the lower value reached after proteins with a distant structural relative are excluded; this adjustment was applied only to the target set and does not place it below the other two.
